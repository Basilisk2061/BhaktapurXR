### Overview

Above the plaza, King Bhupatindra Malla sits with joined hands on an ornamented capital. A slender stone column lifts the figure above the surrounding activity, while a small parasol completes the silhouette. This is a royal monument rather than a shrine or a decorative pillar. A local heritage account describes the king facing toward the Golden Gate and Taleju precinct in devotion. The posture is the key to understanding the sculpture: royal presence is expressed through a seated figure in prayer, not through a standing military portrait.

### History

The monument represents Bhupatindra Malla. Its devotional interpretation connects the king with Taleju and the palace entrance. The sources used here do not securely establish the installation year, so a frequently repeated exact date is not presented as settled history. Nor is an uncorroborated story about damage treated as fact.

The important historical relationship is between the ruler, his posture and the sacred royal setting. The statue belongs to the same forecourt as the Golden Gate, but it remains an independent monument with its own form and viewpoint.

Claim ID: `malla-column:history`. Source IDs: `local-column`. Locator: Bhupatindra identification and Taleju devotion; installation date remains unknown.

### Architecture

The tall stone shaft carries a broad, decorated lotus-like capital and a seated metal figure. Folded legs establish the seated pose; the torso and joined hands make the human silhouette legible. Above the head, a thin pole supports the parasol.

The contrast between the long, restrained shaft and the concentrated ornament at its summit is especially distinctive. The licensed close photograph makes the capital, seat and canopy easier to study than they are from the ground.

Claim ID: `malla-column:architecture`. Source IDs: `photo:malla-column`, `local-column`. Locator: Capital, seated figure, folded legs, hands, pole and parasol.

### Significance

The image presents a ruler in an attitude of devotion. In the local account, the direction toward the Golden Gate reinforces the connection with Taleju. It is a reminder that royal representation in the square can take a religious form as well as an architectural one. Exact meanings beyond this documented devotional relationship are not assigned to individual ornaments.

Claim ID: `malla-column:significance`. Source IDs: `local-column`. Locator: Attributed devotional interpretation and Golden Gate orientation.

### story

Above the plaza, King Bhupatindra Malla sits with joined hands on an ornamented capital. A slender stone column lifts the figure above the surrounding activity, while a small parasol completes the silhouette. This is a royal monument rather than a shrine or a decorative pillar. A local heritage account describes the king facing toward the Golden Gate and Taleju precinct in devotion. The posture is the key to understanding the sculpture: royal presence is expressed through a seated figure in prayer, not through a standing military portrait.

The sitter is Bhupatindra Malla, but the commissioning history is disputed. Slusser calls a commission by the king himself probable yet undocumented, and records the tradition assigning it to Ranjit Malla. NTB Cultural Treasures gives Ranjit and 1753. The guide retains both positions without claiming a settled installation year.

The firm historical relationship is between the ruler, his prayer posture and the sacred palace setting. It is an independent royal monument with its own form, not a temple or part of the Golden Gate.

### purpose

The column presents royal devotion publicly. Its height gives the king's image a lasting presence, but the seated pose and joined hands express reverence rather than physical action. The viewer's eye is led upward from a restrained shaft to a much richer group of forms at its summit.

A commemorative reading fits the tradition of a son's commission, yet that tradition does not establish an exact founding occasion. The sources used here do not justify assigning a hidden symbolic meaning to every ornament on the capital or canopy.

### patron

The sitter is securely identified as Bhupatindra Malla; the commissioner is less certain. Slusser considers a commission by Bhupatindra probable but explicitly undocumented, and records the tradition assigning it to Ranjit, his son. NTB's Cultural Treasures booklet gives Ranjit and 1753.

These are differing claims, not an independently reconciled installation date. The guide therefore identifies the represented ruler with confidence while leaving the act of commissioning qualified. A popular exact year is not a substitute for checked inscriptional evidence.

Claim ID: `malla-column:book:patron`. Source IDs: ntb-world, ntb-cultural, slusser82, local-column, photo:malla-column. Locator: NTB World Heritage booklet p8; Cultural Treasures Bhaktapur paragraph; Slusser volume I p207 indexed excerpt; existing licensed statue detail.

### culturalRole

NTB relates the prayer posture to Taleju Bhawani. Kingship entered the square's religious setting not only through large buildings, but also through an image of a ruler as a devotee. That relationship remains legible even if a visitor cannot identify the person from his appearance alone.

The devotional interpretation is firmer than the stories sometimes told about damage to the column. An uncorroborated bullet-damage anecdote is not repeated here. The close photograph is used to study the figure, capital and parasol, not to invent a missing repair chronology.
